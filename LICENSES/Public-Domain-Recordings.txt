Public domain dedications: the gramophone records released by their performers

Two of the recordings the lobby gramophone plays are dedicated to the public domain by the
people who made them, as stated on the recordings' own Wikimedia Commons file pages and in the
permission Musopen gave. This file records the words, so the licence is on file and not only
linked. The recordings and the page revisions are listed in
apps/web/src/audio/music/records/THIRD_PARTY.md.

1. Gymnopedie No. 1, performed by Robin Alciatore for Musopen
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Erik_Satie_-_gymnopedies_-_la_1_ere._lent_et_douloureux.ogg

   The page carries the template PD-author for the performer, which renders as:

     "This work has been released into the public domain by its author, Robin Alciatore.
      This applies worldwide. In some countries this may not be legally possible; if so:
      Robin Alciatore grants anyone the right to use this work for any purpose, without any
      conditions, unless such conditions are required by law."

2. At the Fireside (Kinderszenen, Op. 15, No. 8), a Musopen recording
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Robert_Schumann_-_scenes_from_childhood,_op._15_-_viii._at_the_fireside.ogg

   The page carries the template PD-author for Musopen, which renders as the same dedication
   with "Musopen" as the author, and a permission ticket (2008012110017088).

Both pages quote the permission that Musopen published with these recordings (its FAQ of the
time, http://www.musopen.com/faq.html):

  "The music on this site is given a public domain license, therefore, there are technically
   no restrictions on what you do with it. We do ask, out of courtesy, two things. One, that you
   do not directly sell our recordings for profit, as a great amount of work has been donated to
   this project, and for the benefit of the public, not profit. Two, we ask that any commercial
   or derived works attribute Musopen somewhere, to give credit to this project."

The project meets the request for attribution: THIRD_PARTY.md names Musopen and the performer
for every record, and the lobby gramophone's captions name the piece and its composer.

The compositions are in the public domain in their own right: each composer died before 1955 and
each work was first published before 1931.
